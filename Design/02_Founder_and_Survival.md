# 02 — Founder & Survival

The early game is about one person, one laptop, one bank balance and 21 blocks of time per week.

## Decisions
- **How do I spend this week?** Development versus gigs versus marketing versus learning versus rest, 21 blocks at a time.
- **How hard do I push?** Extra hours ship sooner but compound into burnout, bugs and collapse.
- **Where do I live?** A cheaper home extends runway at a cost to morale, focus or pride.
- **Which skills do I grow?** Leveling is slow. Specializing is forced. Perk forks are permanent.
- **When do I stop doing the craft myself?** The founder's hour becomes more valuable as a leader than as a maker, but only if someone else can do the craft.

## 4. The day: three slots, spent one at a time

Every day has three **slots** of 4 hours: Morning, Afternoon and Evening. **Time never runs on its own.** Nothing happens until the player spends the current slot, and the day only ends when the player goes to sleep. There is no pause button, no speed setting and no "advance to next event": the player's choices are the clock.

*Changed after the first M1 playtest.* M1 shipped with a real-time clock (pause, 1×, 2×, 4×, "Next week") and a 21-block Week Planner. The first weeks flew past before the player understood anything, and work was a number going up. Now each work session is played as a short minigame, so the player feels the game being built.

| Activity | How it plays | What it does | Trains |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Build** (pick a feature) | **Wire It Up**: turn tiles until the flow reaches every module of the feature. The board dresses up as the work: a circuit (Programming), paint pipes (Art), patch cables (Audio), paths through a level (Design), red string on a corkboard (Writing). Tiny projects bolt two tiles in place; bigger ones have wobbly tiles that keep turning until the flow reaches them (not in relaxed mode). | Project work (05) | The feature's discipline |
| **Polish** (once every feature is built) | **Bug Squash**: click the bugs before they escape | Fixes bugs, adds polish (05) | Programming, Design |
| **Gig work** (pick a gig) | A minigame for the gig's skill: **Bug Squash** (Programming), **Pixel Match** (Art), **Typo Hunt** (Writing), **Synth Tuner** (Audio), **Feedback Sort** (Design) | Gig progress, money and rating (03) | The gig's discipline |
| **Look for clients** | Instant | 50% chance of a new offer | Business |
| **Marketing** (pick a post) | An instant decision: **screenshots** (steady, best for wishlists), **devlog** (reliable, more followers, fewer wishlists), **meme** (same average reach, but usually flops and sometimes explodes) | Followers and wishlists (09) | Marketing |
| **Research** | Instant | Ideas and Knowledge (04) | Design (small) |
| **Learn** (pick a skill) | Instant | XP ×1.25 (×1.5 with a course), no output | The chosen skill |
| **Rest** | Instant | Burnout recovery | — |
| **Day job** (fixed slots) | Forced | Salary on the 1st. Sessions later that day count ×0.8 (tired evenings). | — |

Later activities (Network, Admin, Manage) join the list as the studio grows (07, 08).

- **Playing well matters, but never decides everything.** A minigame's score (0–1) becomes the session's **work multiplier**, from ×0.5 to ×1.2. A score of about 0.7 is an ordinary session (×1.0), which is what the balance bots play. Careful sessions also make better features (task quality +1.5 × (multiplier − 1)) and happier clients (score +2.5 × (average multiplier − 1)). A bad session still gets something done.
- **Minigames grow with the player.** More founder skill gives more time (up to +10 s). Bigger projects and more demanding clients make the board larger or the client pickier.
- **Planning activities are instant on purpose.** Marketing, research, learning and rest are decisions, not dexterity, so they resolve at once with a short note. Only *work* is played.
- **Forced slots:** rest days (a vacation or a collapse), the day job, a jam weekend and a promised night out take their slot whatever you choose. A whole day off is a single "Rest today" click.
- **Tired evenings:** a session after a day-job shift on the same day counts ×0.8. A job takes energy as well as hours, which is why keeping a full-time job never finishes a Small game (13 §12).
- **Flow:** sessions in a row on the same work (the same discipline of your game, the same gig, the same skill) build a streak: +5% output for the second, +10% for the third, +15% from the fourth on. Resting keeps the streak, even overnight; any other work breaks it. The day panel shows a flame badge on whatever would keep it going. *Decision:* stay in the zone, or switch to what's urgent.
- **Coffee:** click the mug in your room to brew one. The next work session that day counts ×1.15, for +1 burnout now. Two a day at most, none at night, and it goes cold overnight. A coffee before the evening session takes the edge off a day-job shift (×0.8 × 1.15 ≈ ×0.92). *Decision:* a small boost now for a little burnout later.
- **The cat:** pet it when you're feeling low (morale under 60) for +2 morale, once a day. When you're fine it just purrs. It's a comfort, not a strategy.
- **Your studio's name:** Doug asks for it on the first step of the tutorial (with suggestions), and you can change it from the menu or by clicking the sign above the window. It appears on the sign, your posters and on launch day.
- **Night:** after the evening comes the day's summary and **Sleep**. Sleeping ends the day: sales, deadlines, and on Sunday night the week's burnout and morale. It then starts the next morning: payments, the bills on the 1st, and Monday's offers and events.
- **"Call it a day"** rests the remaining slots.
- **Weekend work** counts extra toward burnout (+0.5 per session): the social cost. Weekday evenings don't, because any week over 40 hours needs them.
- Bots and balance tests still use a repeating 7×3 **week plan** (`PlanTemplates`). A day played slot by slot gives exactly the same result as the same day from the plan (`SlotTests`).

**What a slot decision looks like:** "It's Thursday afternoon. Sam's gig is due Saturday, with two sessions left. The demo needs six sessions before Next Fest. Rent is due in 9 days. Burnout is 52." There is no correct answer, only priorities.

## 5. Burnout, morale and money anxiety

These numbers match `Tools/economy_sim.py`.

**Burnout (0–100)**, weekly change:
- **+0.35** per hour above 45; **−0.5** per hour below 45.
- **+0.5** per weekend work block.
- **+2** per week while runway is under 2 months ("money anxiety": financial stress causes burnout, which reduces output, which increases financial stress).
- Event stress: Launch Week +5, a harsh review wave +3, a conflict +2.

| Burnout | Effect |
|---|---|
| 0–30 | None. |
| 30–60 | Output × (1 − 0.004 per point above 30). Bugs × (1 + 0.01 per point above 30). |
| 60–75 | As above, plus morale −2/week. Warning prompts suggest a vacation. |
| >75 | **8% weekly chance of Collapse:** 2 weeks of forced rest, one health event. |

Recovery: vacation week −22, long weekend −8, sabbatical month −60.

**Rules of thumb the player can discover:**
- 48-hour weeks (12 blocks) add about +1 per week, which is roughly one vacation a year.
- 60-hour weeks add about +5 per week, reaching burnout 60 in ~11 weeks.

The simulator shows that sustained 60-hour weeks ship a game only ~1 month sooner over 3 years, while spending ~28 weeks recovering and multiplying the chance of going broke by 4.6. Short sprints (before Next Fest or a launch) are a tool. Sustained crunch is a trap.

**Morale (0–100)** changes output by ±10% and affects the founder's innovation contribution.
- Up: progress, praise, sales, shipping (+10), Social blocks.
- Down: bad reviews, isolation (no Social or Network blocks for 4 weeks: −2/week), money anxiety, crunch.
- Below 25 it triggers the **"Crisis of faith"** event: take a day job, talk to friends (costs blocks), or push on (risky).

## 6. Founder skills

**10 skills:**
- **Craft:** Programming, Design, Art, Audio, Writing.
- **Studio:** Business, Marketing, Leadership, Negotiation, Production.

**Leveling (1–10, with decimal progress).** One hour of practice = 1 XP. Going from level L to L+1 costs **70 × L² hours**:

| L2→3 | L3→4 | L4→5 | L5→6 | L6→7 | L7→8 | L8→9 | L9→10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 280 h | 630 h | 1,120 h | 1,750 h | 2,520 h | 3,430 h | 4,480 h | 5,670 h |

Reaching level 7 from level 2 takes about 6,300 hours: 5–6 years of focused practice. **Nobody maxes everything,** so choosing what to grow is a real decision.

- **Learning:** free tutorials ×1.2 XP; paid courses ($50–$400) ×1.5; bootcamps ($2,000 for 4 weeks) ×2.0. Learning produces no project output.
- **Mentors** (network contacts): ×1.25 XP in their skill while you meet them (1 block every 2 weeks).
- **Intrinsic effects per level:**
  - Craft: work efficiency (0.55 + 0.09 × level) and task quality (05).
  - Business: forecast accuracy, admin time, grant and contract quality.
  - Marketing: content performance, +6% per level.
  - Leadership: management hours per employee, −4% per level.
  - Negotiation: ask success chance.
  - Production: estimate calibration and coordination overhead, −3% per level.
- **Judgment:** you can only evaluate work up to about **your skill + 2** in that discipline. An Art 2 founder sees an artist's portfolio as a wide range. Weak areas stay foggy until you hire someone good or learn (17).

## 8. From maker to leader: the founder's role

- **Management overhead:** each direct report costs 1.5 founder hours per week (1:1s, reviews, unblocking), −4% per Leadership level. Leads and a Producer absorb it (07).
- **At Tier 3 the founder chooses a role.** It can be changed once every 2 years, at a morale cost to whoever is affected.
  - **Creative Director:** keeps up to half their slots on craft for one project. +Vision cohesion and Innovation on that project. Management must be delegated.
  - **Studio Head:** no craft slots on projects. Unlocks more strategic actions per month and better publisher and investor relations.
  - **Hybrid:** both at reduced strength, with higher burnout risk.
- **Strategic actions** (late game) also cost founder slots:

| Action | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Creative review of a project | 2 slots | Reveals true pillar estimates; small quality gain |
| Pitch or deal meeting | 2 slots | Required for negotiations |
| Conference | 5–10 slots | Contacts, deals, press |
| Key hire interview | 1 slot | Reveals candidate traits |
| Investor or board meeting | 2 slots | Keeps investor relations healthy |
| Crisis response | Varies | Resolves the crisis |

This is how the early game's "three slots a day" becomes the late game's "limited attention" without introducing a new currency.

## 9. Personal life (light, optional)

Setting: *Personal events: On / Minimal / Off.* Off means none, and nothing is penalized.

- **Anchors:** Relationships and Health. Months without social time create strain events. Attending them costs slots and restores morale.
- **Examples:**
  - "Your best friend's wedding is on launch weekend."
  - "Your parents offer a $5,000 loan, interest-free, but they'll ask about it every holiday."
  - "Your back hurts. Buy a proper chair ($400) or ignore it." (Ignoring it raises burnout accumulation until fixed.)
  - "An old colleague offers you a senior job at $95k/year." (A real exit, and a real test of commitment.)

## 10. The first weeks: Doug's tutorial, then events

**Day one is a guided tutorial.** Old Dev Doug, the veteran developer who mentors you, walks through it one step at a time:

1. Naming your studio.
2. The goal in the HUD (Chapter 1: ship your first game).
3. The runway.
4. The three slots of the day.
5. Opening GigGrid and accepting Sam's gig.
6. Playing a gig session.
7. Starting a Tiny project and building its first feature.
8. The 45-hour rule.
9. Going to sleep.

How the tutorial behaves:
- Steps that ask for an action finish when you've done it.
- A pulsing ring points at what to click, and the window scrolls to it.
- It can be skipped at any time.

Afterwards Doug stays in the corner of the room with situational advice ("your first estimate is always wrong"). The tutorial is a coach, not a script: the simulation runs normally underneath it.

Onboarding then continues through events:

| Week | Event (inbox) | System introduced |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Doug's tutorial. "Day one." Rent reminder. Sam's GigGrid gig: fix bugs, 12 hours, $180. | Day slots, minigames, gigs, runway, the chapter goal |
| 1 | Two starter ideas are in your notebook. The first Tiny project starts. | Idea Notebook, features, Wire It Up |
| 2–3 | Every feature is built. Open a store page ($100 Vapor fee) or go free on scratch.io? | Store page, pricing, polish |
| 3–4 | Launch the tiny game. Chapter 1 is complete. | Launch, Chapter 2 |
| 5 | Rent is paid on the 1st. First monthly report. | Finance basics |
| 5–6 | A 72-hour jam is announced (optional). | Game jams |
| 6–8 | The laptop overheats: buy a $120 cooling pad or risk it. | Hardware (problem first) |
| 8 | The first post-mortem. The first Lessons and Knowledge entries. | Knowledge, post-mortems |

Later systems unlock on triggers (see the unlock schedule in 16).
