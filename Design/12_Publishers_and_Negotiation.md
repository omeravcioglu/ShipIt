# 12 — Publishers, Platform Deals & Negotiation

Publishers trade **money and services now** for **revenue share, deadlines, rights and sometimes control later.** The terms outlast the project: a clause signed in year 3 can decide who makes your sequel in year 9.

## Decisions
- **Self-publish or partner?** And with whom?
- **Which terms matter most to you?** Cash now, revenue share, IP, sequels, platforms, creative control.
- **How hard to negotiate?** Push too far and the deal walks away. Push too little and you pay for years.
- **Pay for legal review?** Information costs money before you sign.
- **Accept a platform deal** (exclusivity, subscription, funding)? The money is guaranteed; your community may not like it.

## 1. What publishers offer, and what it costs

| They can offer (from the brief) | They typically take |
|---|---|
| Development funding (advances) | A revenue share (25–60% after recoup, often 100% before) |
| Marketing budget | Recoupment of the advance and often of marketing, porting and localization costs |
| Localization, console porting, QA | Deadlines and milestones with penalties |
| Influencer marketing, PR | Rights: platforms, DLC, sequels |
| Platform connections, featuring | Sometimes the IP |
| — | Sometimes creative control |

## 3. Finding a publisher: pitching

**Channels:**

| Channel | Cost | Response rate |
|---|---|---|
| Cold email | 1 block | 5–15% |
| Conference meetings (DevCon, PlayExpo) | Event cost + blocks | 30–60% |
| Introduction through a contact (14) | A favor | 40–70% |
| **Inbound** | — | Publishers approach *you* after strong wishlist velocity, a festival hit or a viral moment |
| Festival scouting | — | Scouts watch Next Fest and showcases |

**The pitch kit:**
- **Pitch deck** (Business and Writing quality).
- **Vertical slice or demo** (quality).
- **Trailer.**
- **Budget and schedule.** Production skill decides credibility: *asking for too little is a red flag.*
- **Market analysis** (Market Knowledge).
- **Team credibility:** shipped games, reputation.

**The publisher's evaluation is hidden.** It weighs slice quality, fit with their portfolio, traction (wishlists, followers), team credibility, budget realism and market timing.

**Outcomes:**
- **Rejection with feedback.** This is valuable information: *"The art is stunning, but the core loop isn't clear yet."* It reveals a weakness in hidden pillars.
- **"Show us the next build in 8 weeks."**
- **A term sheet.**

Pitching costs founder blocks. A pitch roadshow can take 2–3 months, which itself affects the schedule.

## 4. Deal terms

| Term | Range and notes |
|---|---|
| **Advance / development funding** | Paid in **milestone tranches**: for example 15% on signing, 10–15% per milestone, 10% at launch. |
| **Recoupment** | The advance (and often marketing, porting and localization) is recovered from revenue before the developer earns royalties. |
| **Revenue split** | Before recoup: often 70–100% to the publisher. After recoup: 25–60% to the publisher. |
| **Marketing commitment** | A guaranteed minimum spend ("up to" is not a guarantee). |
| **Services** | QA, localization, porting, PR, influencers, platform relations, community management. |
| **IP ownership** | Developer / shared / publisher. |
| **Sequel rights** | None / first negotiation / first refusal / owned by the publisher. |
| **Platform rights** | PC only / including consoles / all platforms. |
| **DLC revenue** | Same split / a different split / controlled by the publisher. |
| **Deadline and milestones** | Deliverables, dates, and penalties (late fees, withheld tranches, termination rights). |
| **Creative control** | Consultation / approval on key decisions / the publisher can mandate changes. |
| **Exclusivity** | Platform exclusivity windows. |
| **Term and territory** | How long, and where. |
| **Audit rights** | Your right to check royalty statements. |
| **Payment terms** | Royalties quarterly, often 45 days after the quarter ends (13). |
| **Termination** | For cause; **for convenience** (dangerous); kill fee; **IP reversion** if cancelled. |
| **Cross-collateralization** | Losses on one game recouped from another. A hidden danger in multi-game deals. |

## 5. Recoupment math: the brief's two offers

- **Offer A:** advance $150,000; publisher 35% after recoup; **developer keeps the IP**; marketing $60,000 (recoupable). Recoup total: $210,000.
- **Offer B:** advance $400,000; publisher 50% after recoup; **publisher owns the IP**; marketing included (not recouped). Recoup total: $400,000.

Developer royalties by net revenue *R* (after platform fees):

| Net revenue R | Offer A: 0.65 × (R − 210k) | Offer B: 0.50 × (R − 400k) | Self-published (100%, but you fund everything) |
|---|---|---|---|
| $300k (modest) | $58.5k | $0 | $300k (minus your own dev + marketing spend) |
| $1M (good) | $513.5k | $300k | $1M |
| $3M (hit) | $1.81M | $1.30M | $3M |
| $10M (phenomenon) | $6.36M | $4.80M + **no sequel control** | $10M |

**What the table doesn't show:**
- B's extra $250k can fund a bigger, better game that sells more.
- B is **insurance:** if the game flops, you still built it with someone else's money.
- A keeps your **IP**: every sequel, port and licensing deal stays yours (11).
- Self-publishing means *you* carry the development and marketing risk and cash-flow gaps.

There's no universally correct choice, which is what makes it a meaningful decision.

## 6. Milestones and payments

- Publisher **milestone reviews** check the build against the milestone definition:
  - **Pass:** the tranche is paid.
  - **Partial:** partial payment or an extension.
  - **Fail:** payment is withheld, with a warning.
  - **Repeated failure:** the publisher gains a termination right.
- Milestones mirror internal phases (vertical slice, alpha, beta, gold). **Vague milestone definitions are dangerous for you;** clear ones protect you. A Producer and Production skill write better definitions during negotiation.
- Because payments come in tranches, **a missed milestone is a cash-flow crisis,** not just a schedule slip (13).

## 7. Hidden clauses and legal review

- Contracts can contain **hidden clauses**; the chance depends on the publisher's honesty:
  - Cross-collateralization.
  - Marketing recouped at 1.25×.
  - Termination for convenience with no kill fee.
  - Sequel first refusal for 2 games.
  - "All platforms" rights slipped in.
  - Missing audit rights.
  - A 90-day royalty statement delay.
  - "The publisher may alter the game."
- **Ways to reveal them:**

| Method | Cost | Reveals |
|---|---|---|
| Lawyer review | $1,500–5,000 | All of them |
| Legal templates (08) | $300 | About 30% |
| *Legal Eye* perk (Business L7) | — | About 50% |
| Network gossip about the publisher | A favor | Their *typical* clauses |
| Past experience with the publisher | — | What they did last time |

- **The decision:** spending $3,000 you don't have to read a contract you might sign anyway. Players who skip it sometimes learn why lawyers exist.

## 8. Negotiation

**Your leverage (0–100)** comes from:
- Traction: wishlists, festival results, buzz.
- Vertical slice quality and studio reputation.
- **Competing offers** (the strongest source).
- Relationship, and market timing (a hot genre helps).
- **Cash runway.** Desperation reduces leverage: under 3 months of runway is −20, and publishers can tell.

**The publisher's side:**
- **Patience:** 3–6 rounds of goodwill.
- **Hidden priorities:** a weight per term, for example "values IP highly, flexible on marketing".
- **Hidden walk-away limits** per term.

**Moves:**

| Move | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Ask** | Push one term toward your target. Success = f(distance to their limit, leverage, Negotiation skill, relationship). Failure costs patience. Asking beyond their limit always fails and costs double. |
| **Trade** | Give on a term they value to gain on one you value. *Win-Win* makes trades 30% more effective. |
| **Package** | Propose several terms at once. Accepted if their total value clears their threshold. |
| **Request time** | To collect competing bids. They may impose an exploding deadline. |
| **Walk away** | With high leverage, they may come back with a better final offer. Otherwise they move on. |
| **Bluff** | Claim a competing offer. Success scales with leverage and skill. If they check through their network and it's false: relationship −, and Publisher Reputation −. |

**Skill hooks:**
- *Read the Room* (L3): shows patience and rough priorities.
- **L4:** publishers open with 5% better revenue splits (the brief's example).
- *Hard Bargainer* versus *Win-Win* (L5 fork).
- *Walk-Away Power* (L7).
- *Rights Keeper* versus *Big Check* (L9 fork).

**The UI:** a table of terms, each showing their offer, your ask, and colored zones (green likely, yellow uncertain, red likely rejected). **The accuracy of the zones depends on skill**, so beginners negotiate partly blind.

**Negotiable points** (from the brief): revenue split, advance, marketing budget, deadline, IP ownership, sequels, platform exclusivity, DLC revenue, console rights. Also milestone definitions, the kill fee and IP reversion.

## 9. Living with a publisher

- **Creative requests:** *"Add microtransactions." "Add a battle pass." "Cut the story." "Add multiplayer."*
  - **Comply:** quality and trust risk.
  - **Negotiate:** costs patience.
  - **Refuse:** relationship −. If they hold approval rights, the conflict escalates.
- **Marketing underdelivery:** their true marketing strength is hidden until you've seen it.
- **The publisher cancels the project** (portfolio strategy, poor milestones, a market shift). Your kill fee and IP reversion, if you negotiated them, decide whether you can find another partner or self-publish. This is the brief's "Publisher cancels project" event.
- **Publisher bankruptcy:** unpaid royalties and rights in limbo (a legal event chain).
- **Royalty statements** arrive quarterly. **Audit rights** catch "errors", which are more common with low-honesty publishers.
- **Good partnerships compound:** first-look deals, better terms next time, inbound offers, platform introductions.

## 10. Platform deals

| Deal | How it works | Upside | Shadow |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Epoch exclusivity** | A **minimum guarantee (MG)** based on wishlists, buzz and genre, for 12 months of PC exclusivity. 50% paid at signing, 50% at launch. Epoch sales count against the MG. | Guaranteed money. The risk transfers to Epoch. | Community backlash, especially if backers were promised Vapor keys (a broken promise). Review-bomb risk when it reaches Vapor. Vapor wishlists go stale for 12 months. |
| **Axis Pass** | A flat fee (roughly 10–30% of projected lifetime platform revenue) for day-one inclusion for 12–24 months | Money now. A population boost for multiplayer games. Word of mouth. | Fewer sales on Axis, and some on PC |
| **Platform funding / timed exclusivity** (Nimbus Indie Fund, Vertex Partners) | Funding for a timed console exclusive | Porting support, featuring | Other platforms wait |
| **Featuring** | Platform representatives (contacts) choose games to feature | Sales spikes | Earned through good ports, reliable certification and events |
| **Porting partners** | A porting studio for a 15–25% revenue share, or a fee | Consoles without in-house expertise | Less control and margin |

## 11. Self-publishing versus a publisher

| | Self-publish | Publisher |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue kept | 100% (after platform fees) | 40–75% after recoup, often 0% before |
| Risk | All yours | Shared or transferred |
| Services | Build or buy them yourself | Included (quality varies) |
| Cash flow | You fund everything | Tranches, if you hit milestones |
| Rights and IP | Yours | Negotiated |
| Knowledge gained | Marketing and business skills grow | Less learning; more focus on development |
| Speed to scale | Slow | Fast |

## 12. Becoming a publisher (late game)

At Tier 4 (or earlier with *Portfolio Thinker*), the studio can launch a **publishing label** (15, 16). You scout pitches from generated studios whose game quality is **hidden from you**: the information problem from the other side of the table. You offer deals, provide services from your in-house capabilities, and manage a portfolio. Your **reputation as a publisher** (developer-friendly or predatory) decides the quality of the pitches you receive. What you learned signing your own deals shapes how you treat others.
