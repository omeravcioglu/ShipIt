# 14 — Reputation, Network, Market, Competitors & Events

The studio doesn't exist in a vacuum. Reputation is a set of memories other people hold about you. The market moves. Rivals act. Events arrive, but they're **conditioned on your situation**, not rolled at random.

## Decisions
- **Which reputation do I invest in?** You can't be everyone's favorite at once.
- **Who do I spend network time on?** Relationships decay without care.
- **Which trends do I chase, and which do I ignore?**
- **How do I respond to rivals:** compete, avoid, or collaborate?
- **How do I respond to events** that change the ground under a project?

---

## 1. Reputation dimensions

The brief's five dimensions, plus **Brand Awareness**. Awareness isn't the same as reputation: a studio can be famous and distrusted.

| Dimension | Held by | Raised by | Lowered by | Affects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Developer Reputation** (critical, quality) | Critics, press, award juries | Review scores (recent weighted more), awards, innovation | Bad releases, asset flips | Press coverage, nominations, publisher interest. **Expectations rise.** |
| **Player Trust** | Players | Kept promises, support, fair pricing, transparency | Broken promises, abandoned games, predatory monetization, misleading marketing | Fan conversion, review generosity, crowdfunding and Early Access, resilience to review bombs |
| **Publisher Reputation** (as a partner) | Publishers, platforms | Milestones met, professionalism, honest negotiation, successful deals | Missed milestones, **bluffs caught**, disputes | Deal terms, inbound offers, platform featuring |
| **Employee Reputation** (Gamedoor rating) | Workers | Fair pay, no crunch, growth, good culture, layoffs handled humanely | Crunch, layoffs, tolerating toxic staff, low pay | Candidate pool, salary asks, poaching resistance |
| **Industry Reputation** (prestige with peers) | Developers, event organizers | Awards, talks, collaborations, helping others, success | Scandals, aggressive poaching, copying | Networking, conference invitations, collaboration offers, platform relations |
| **Brand Awareness** | Gamers | Releases, marketing, virality, franchises | Slow decay over time | Baseline wishlists; fans following you across genres (09) |

- Each dimension runs **0–100** and is shown as a named band (*Unknown → Noticed → Respected → Renowned → Legendary*) with a trend arrow.
- **Fame cuts both ways.** High Awareness amplifies good news *and* scandals. High Developer Reputation raises expectations (05).

## 2. Reputation tags (patterns of behavior)

Tags are how the world summarizes you. This is the brief's *"a studio famous for abandoning games may have trouble selling future games."*

| Tag | Earned by | Effect | Removed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Delivers on Promises** | 5 promises kept and none broken in 2 years | Trust gains ×1.2; crowdfunding +20% | Breaking a major promise |
| **Broken Promises** | 3 broken promises in 2 years, or a failed crowdfunding delivery | Trust gains ×0.7; crowdfunding −40% | 2 years of kept promises |
| **Abandons Games** | Sunsetting 2 games within 12 months of launch, or abandoning Early Access | Early Access and new-game conversion −20% | Supporting the next game for 12+ months |
| **Asset Flipper** | 2 releases with a high asset-flip index | Innovation −1 baseline; harsher critics | 2 original releases |
| **Crunch Studio** | 3 crunch periods in 2 years | Candidate pool −30%, poaching +, press risk | 2 years without crunch |
| **Great Place to Work** | Gamedoor ≥4.2 for 2 years | Candidate pool +30%, salary asks −5% | Crunch or layoffs |
| **Genre Master (X)** | 3 well-reviewed games in genre X | Genre fans convert +25%; **expectations in X rise** | — |
| **Performance Kings** | 3 games with Performance ≥8 | Tech outlets favor you; Deck and console featuring | A broken launch |
| **Broken Launches** | 2 launches with crash or critical bug waves | Launch-week conversion −15% ("wait for patches") | 2 clean launches |
| **Community First** | High trust and an active community for 2 years | Review bombs have half the effect | A controversy |
| **Service Studio** | More than 60% of capacity on contracts for 12 months | Contracts easier; creative candidates harder; audience forgets you | 2 releases of your own |
| **Darling of the Press** | Awards + sustained coverage | Coverage chance +; expectations rise | A flop |
| **One-Hit Wonder** | A hit followed by 2 flops | Publishers cautious; press angle "can they do it again?" | Another hit |
| **Great Ports** | 3 well-rated console ports | Platform featuring; porting contracts | A broken port |

## 3. The industry network (contacts)

- **Contact types:** developers, publisher business-development reps, journalists, creators, platform representatives, investors, recruiters, event organizers, vendors, mentors, and **alumni** (ex-employees).
- **Relationship (0–100):**
  - Grows through interactions: events, collaborations, favors, playing their games, helping them.
  - **Decays without contact:** −1/month after 6 months of silence.
- **What contacts give:**

| Benefit | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Gossip** (information) | Reveals hidden info: which publishers pay late, typical contract clauses, a rival's upcoming announcement, trend insight |
| **Introductions** | To publishers, investors, creators |
| **Favors** (spend relationship) | A journalist covers you; a creator plays your demo; a platform rep features you |
| **Referrals** | Hires (07) |
| **Collaborations** | Bundles, cross-promotion, shared booths, co-development |
| **Mentorship** | XP boost (02) |

- **Memory:** contacts remember how you treated them: a bluff that was called, a promise kept, an unpaid invoice, a kind word after their launch flopped.
- Networking is **a time investment with delayed payoff.** The founder's Network blocks, conferences and jams compete directly with development.

## 4. The market

- **Genre demand:** `D_g(t) = base_g × trend(t) × platform mix × macro economy`.
- **Trend lifecycle:** Emerging → Rising → Peak → Saturated → Declining → Niche/Cult (and sometimes a revival).
  - Phases last 6–36 months. Trends are **triggered by breakout hits** (yours or an AI studio's).
  - Rising trends attract **clones** (AI studios pivot), which speeds up saturation.
- **Saturation:** the recent releases in a genre, weighted by visibility (half-life 6 months). The effective market for a release is roughly `D_g / (1 + k × S_g)`. The formula is hidden; the player sees saturation bands.
- **Themes** trend too (Cozy, Cosmic Horror, Cyberpunk…), with smaller swings. **Evergreen genres** (strategy, RPG, simulation) barely swing.
- **Graphics-expectation drift:** every year, expectations rise by about +0.2 Graphics points for realistic 3D, +0.1 for stylized 3D, and +0.03 for 2D, pixel and stylized 2D. A long project in realistic 3D **ages during development**; stylized games barely age. This also creates remaster opportunities (11).
- **Platform lifecycle:** new platforms launch (for example "Nimbus 2") with a **launch window**: small but hungry audiences and generous featuring for early games. Old platforms decline.
- **Seasonality:** Q4 has more spending and more competition. The summer sale. The January lull.
- **Macro economy:** Normal / Boom / Recession cycles lasting years.
  - **Recession:** lower price tolerance (−10–20% conversion at premium prices). Publishers and VCs tighten. **Industry layoffs flood the talent pool** (cheaper senior hires). It cuts both ways.
  - **Boom:** the reverse: inflated salaries and more competition.
- **The Market Report:** trend phase per genre and theme with arrows, saturation bands, median revenue (GameStatz) and a 12–24 month forecast band. **Its accuracy depends on how fresh your Market Knowledge is** (it decays over ~6 months, 17).

## 5. Competitors

About 40 AI studios exist at the start. They spawn and die over time, ranging from solo developers to AAA publishers.

| Archetype | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Cloner** | Jumps on trends. Fast, low quality. Saturates genres. |
| **Artisan** | Slow, high quality, niche. |
| **Hype Machine** | Big marketing, uneven quality. |
| **AAA Titan** | Mega-releases that dominate launch windows. Occasionally shadow-drops. |
| **Scrappy Upstart** | Could become your rival or your friend. |
| **Publisher-backed Mid** | Steady output. |
| **Rising Star** | Always a few steps ahead of you. A **visible benchmark** and a natural rival. |

- **What they do:** announce games (the release calendar), release games (generated review scores; GameStatz sales estimates that are ±30–50% off), grow and shrink, hire and lay off (which changes the talent pool), get acquired and go bankrupt.
- **Rivalries** form from competing in the same genre or launch window, poaching each other's staff, public spats, or award competition. Rival AI **reacts to your success**: it clones your hit genre, targets your staff, and schedules releases near yours.
- **Friendships** bring collaboration offers (bundles, cross-promotion, shared booths, co-development), playtest swaps, referrals and gossip.
- **Acquisitions:** late in the game you can buy struggling small studios (a team plus their IP), with culture-clash risk (07).
- **Watching a Rising Star teaches:** *"They reached 50k wishlists in 3 months. How?"* Their public actions (festivals, creators, trailer timing) are visible in the Market screen.

## 6. "A similar game has been announced" (the brief's scenario)

- **Trigger:** a rival announces a game very similar to yours (genre, theme and hook) launching near your date. It's more likely when your idea is generic and the trend is hot.
- **Information shown:** the rival's profile, hype, estimated quality (a range) and launch window.

| Option (from the brief, plus two) | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Rush development** | Move the date up and launch first; quality drops |
| **Delay and improve** | Let them go first, learn from their reviews. Risk: their fans are already satisfied. |
| **Change marketing** | Differentiate the message, emphasize your hook, target a different audience |
| **Change features** | Pivot the hook or add a distinguishing feature (scope creep) |
| **Collaborate** | Cross-promote ("If you liked theirs, try ours"). Needs a friendly relationship. |
| **Ignore** | Trust your quality |

The outcome depends on relative hype, quality and hook clarity. Sometimes **their launch helps** (attention to the genre) if yours is better. Sometimes **they launch broken**, and their audience comes looking for an alternative.

## 7. Industry events

**Design rules:**
1. **Conditioned on state.** Events react to what you did and where you are.
2. **Foreshadowed** when possible: rumors come first.
3. **Always offer choices.**
4. **Rarely purely good or bad.**
5. **Cooldowns and chains**, so events don't repeat and they build stories.
6. **Scale with tier.** Bedroom events are personal; late events are strategic.

| Category | Event (the brief's list, plus more) | Typical condition | Choices |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market | **A genre becomes trendy** | A breakout hit | Pivot / accelerate / ignore |
| Market | **Competitor releases a similar game** | Genericness, trend heat | See §6 |
| Market | AAA mega-release lands near your date | Calendar | Move the date / hold |
| Market | **Economic recession** | Macro cycle | Cut costs / hold / invest while talent is cheap |
| Market | **Advertising prices increase** | Q4 or a platform change | Shift to organic and creators / pay up |
| Platform | **New platform launches** | Lifecycle | Port early (featuring, small audience) / wait |
| Platform | **Platform algorithm changes** | Cooldown-gated | Adapt your channel mix |
| Platform | Store policy change (fees, content rules, AI disclosure) | — | Comply / protest / shift platforms |
| Platform | A platform invites your game to a showcase | Reputation + quality | Accept (trailer deadline) / decline |
| Technology | **New engine technology appears** | Yearly | Adopt (upgrade cost) / stay (expectations drift) |
| Technology | Engine pricing controversy (Unify per-install fee) | You use Unify | Switch engines mid-project / absorb the cost / join the protest (trust +) |
| Technology | Middleware abandoned | You use it | Migrate / stay |
| People | **Employee quits** | Morale, poaching (07) | Counter / let go |
| People | Industry-wide layoffs flood the talent pool | Macro | Hire seniors cheaply (onboarding capacity!) / hold |
| People | A famous developer wants to join | Reputation | Hire (salary, ego, expectations) / decline |
| Studio / Game | **Major YouTuber plays your game** | Buzz, traits | Discount / update / thank them / ride it |
| Studio / Game | **Game goes viral** | Cascade (09) | Scale servers / capitalize / manage expectations |
| Studio / Game | **Game gets review-bombed** | A controversy (11) | Statement / reverse / hold firm |
| Studio / Game | **Publisher cancels the project** | Portfolio, milestones (12) | New publisher / self-publish / shelve |
| Studio / Game | A build leaks or is pirated early | Release | Respond / add DRM (trust −) / ignore |
| Studio / Game | A glitch clip goes viral (good or bad) | Bugs, physics | Embrace it as a feature / patch it |
| Studio / Game | Award nomination | Quality, innovation | Campaign for it (money, time) / let it be |
| Studio / Game | A speedrun community adopts your game | Traits | Support it (longevity) |
| Studio / Game | A fan game uses your IP | Brand strength | Allow (goodwill) / cease and desist (trust −) |
| Studio / Game | A mobile clone of your game appears | A hit | Legal action (cost) / ignore / make your own mobile version |
| Personal | See 02 | Setting | — |

**Event chains** (multi-step stories):
- **The Poacher:** a rival courts your lead → you counter-offer → months later the rival crunches its team → your lead's former colleagues ask to join you.
- **The Rising Star:** a rival grows fast → announces a game in your genre → you meet at DevCon → you collaborate or compete → they either collapse (their staff become available) or become your defining rival.
- **The Engine Wars:** a pricing controversy → a community protest → an engine migration decision → the engine backs down, or doesn't, and your choice looks prophetic or foolish.
