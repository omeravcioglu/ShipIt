# 07 — Team & People

People are the studio's most powerful and most expensive resource, and the main source of its stories. Every hire raises capacity and also raises burn rate, management load and risk.

## Decisions
- **Whether, when and who to hire:** role, seniority, location, and a salary you can sustain.
- **How much certainty to buy about a candidate:** interviews, work trials, references.
- **How to keep people:** raises, promotions, culture, workload, meaningful projects.
- **How hard to push them:** crunch, deadlines, and what it costs later.
- **How to structure a growing studio:** leads, producers, teams, delegation.
- **Hard calls:** conflicts, counter-offers, layoffs, firing.

## 3. Traits (hidden at hiring, revealed over time)

Each employee has 1–3 traits. Interviews reveal hints, work trials reveal more, and **everything is revealed after 3–6 months of working together** or through events. This makes hiring an information problem, not a shopping list.

| Trait | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Perfectionist** | +1 quality on their tasks, −20% speed. Resists shipping. Delegated cards lean toward quality. |
| **Pragmatist** | Good at cuts. Delegated cards lean toward time. |
| **Genre Fanatic (X)** | +quality and morale on genre X. Morale − on other genres. |
| **Tinkerer** | Proposes rewrites and new tech (more rewrite cards). Loves R&D tasks. |
| **Visionary** | +Innovation. Pitches bold ideas. Clashes with Auteur founders. |
| **Mentor** | Juniors under them gain +30% XP. Softens conflicts. |
| **Natural Leader** | +2 effective Leadership. Wants a lead role within ~18 months. |
| **Ambitious** | Wants promotions fast. Leaves if stuck for 2 years. |
| **Loner** | Output + when remote or in focus rooms. Teamwork − in open offices. |
| **Night Owl** | +output with flexible hours. −output with rigid schedules. |
| **Hothead** | Conflict events. Sometimes a passionate advocate. |
| **Steady** | Low output variance. Slow burnout accumulation. |
| **Streamer** | Streams development (with permission): audience +, occasional leak or drama risk. |
| **Fast Learner** | +40% XP. |
| **Burnout-prone** | Burnout ×1.4. |
| **Loyal Soul** | Loyalty grows ×2. Hard to poach. |
| **Job Hopper** | Loyalty grows ×0.5. Poaching acceptance +. |
| **Diva** | High skill. Morale swings with recognition. Wants credits. |
| **Idea Machine** | Frequent pitches (Personal Passion idea cards). |
| **Networker** | Brings contacts (14). Referral hires. |
| **Documentation Hero** | Bus-factor risk lower. Faster onboarding for others. |

## 4. Hiring

**Channels:**

| Channel | Cost | Speed | Candidate quality | What you know |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Job board post | $300 | 2–4 weeks | Mixed, many applicants | Resume only |
| Referral (employees, contacts) | Free | Varies | Good fit | +1 trait known |
| **Game jam teammates** (03) | Free | When met | Varies | **Everything**: you've worked with them |
| Contractor → hire (06) | Free | Immediate | Known | Skill and reliability known |
| Recruiter | 15–20% of annual salary | 2–6 weeks | Top candidates | Skills narrowed |
| Poaching from a rival | Premium salary | Fast | High | Rival relationship −, possible retaliation |
| Interns | ~$1,500/month (unpaid internships aren't available by design) | Seasonal | Low skill, high potential | Needs mentoring |
| University partnership (Tier 3+) | $5–15k/year | Seasonal | Juniors | Graduate pipeline |

**The candidate pool** (size and quality) depends on:
- Employee Reputation (Gamedoor rating) and studio fame.
- **Project appeal:** people want to work on interesting, well-reviewed games.
- Location or remote policy (08) and salary positioning.
- Engine choice: Unify has a huge talent pool; Forge's is small but enthusiastic.
- Studio tags: "Crunch Studio" shrinks the pool; "Great place to work" grows it.

**Evaluating candidates:**
- **Resume (V):** role, years, salary ask. Skills appear as ranges (E).
- **Interview** (1 block for the founder or a lead): narrows skill ranges by 50% and reveals 1 trait hint.
- **Work trial** (paid $500–1,500, 1 week): reveals true skill and speed.
- **Reference check** (network): reveals reliability.
- **Judgment limit:** evaluators can only narrow ranges up to **their own skill + 2** in that discipline. A great lead programmer can judge programmers. An Art 2 founder can't judge artists. (This is why the first senior hire in each discipline matters so much.)

**Offers:** salary, title, remote or on-site, profit share or equity, start date. Candidates may counter-offer. Top talent may be holding competing offers from rivals, with deadlines.

## 5. The co-founder option (early game)

A jam teammate or contact can become a **co-founder**.
- **Pay:** no salary; they cover their own living costs.
- **Equity:** 20–50%, vesting over 4 years with a 1-year cliff.
- **Upside:** doubles the early team for free. You know their skills and traits.
- **Shadow:**
  - Big decisions need their agreement if they own ≥30%.
  - Creative disagreements, burnout, or a partner who needs them to earn money.
  - If they leave, unvested equity returns, but they may take knowledge or ideas with them.
  - Buy-outs are expensive.

This is one of the most consequential and story-rich decisions in the early game.

## 6. Onboarding and Brooks's law

- New hires ramp from **40% to 100%** productivity over **4–10 weeks**. It's faster with documentation (research), a Mentor, and familiarity with the same engine.
- During onboarding, each new hire consumes **3–5 hours a week** of a senior's time.
- Combined with coordination overhead (05), this means **hiring late in a project can slow it down.** The Concept Forecast and milestone reviews show the net effect once the player has a Producer or Production ≥4.

## 7. Management overhead and span of control

- **Founder:** 1.5 hours per week per direct report (−4% per Leadership level). Ten direct reports eat about 4 blocks a week.
- **Leads** manage 4–7 people well, depending on Leadership. More than that causes "missed handoff" and "duplicate work" events.
- **Without a Producer,** teams of 6+ get "chaos" events and wider estimate ranges.

| Team size | Recommended structure | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Founder manages everyone | Small overhead |
| 4–7 | Founder + a first lead or a Producer | Founder hours |
| 8–15 | Producer + discipline leads | Coordination |
| 16–40 | Several project teams, each with a lead and producer; shared services (QA, audio, marketing) | Parallel projects |
| 40+ | Directors, studio operations, recruiting, finance, publishing | Scale |

## 8. Growth

- **XP:** 1 XP per hour of work in a discipline. The level curve matches the founder's (70 × L² hours per level), so a full-time junior at skill 3 reaches skill 6 in about 2 years. **Potential** caps the maximum.
- **Accelerators:** a training budget (courses, conferences), Mentors, the *Fast Learner* trait, the founder's *Mentor* perk.
- **Promotions:** Junior → Mid → Senior → Lead. Each raises salary expectations.
  - **The Peter principle:** a great programmer can be a poor lead. Leadership is a separate attribute, and it's hidden for first-time leads until they've led.
- **Role changes** tell stories: the QA tester who becomes a designer, the artist who becomes an art director.

## 9. Morale

```
Morale drift/week = fairness (pay vs market and vs peers)
                  + workload (hours, crunch)
                  + meaning (project fits their genre taste and creativity; creative freedom)
                  + success (recent reviews, sales, awards, credits)
                  + environment (office, equipment, remote fit)
                  + recognition (bonuses, promotions, praise events)
                  + relationships (friends at work, conflicts)
                  + management attention (1:1s; neglect causes decay)
                  − uncertainty (layoff rumours, a low runway if finances are open)
```

- **Effects:** output ±10–15%, quality ±0.5, retention, how they resolve delegated cards, and Gamedoor reviews.
- **Visibility:** exact morale is shown as a mood band. Signals ("seems withdrawn") appear first. The founder's *One-on-Ones* perk and HR make it more precise.

## 10. Loyalty and poaching

- **Loyalty** grows with tenure, fair treatment, shared success, equity or profit share, and being kept through a crisis. It drops with broken promises, crunch, being passed over, and layoffs of friends.
- **Poaching attempts** are more likely the more visible someone is (credits on hits, awards, conference talks, streaming), the higher their skill, and the more rivals are hiring.
- **Acceptance chance** rises with the pay gap and ambition, and falls with morale and loyalty.
- **Responses:**
  - **Counter-offer:** costs money. Others may hear about it and expect raises too (fairness).
  - **Appeal:** a Leadership check, stronger with loyalty.
  - **Promote**, if a role exists.
  - **Let them go gracefully:** they join the **alumni network** (a future contact, referral source or collaborator, and sometimes they come back).
- **Bus factor:** if one person holds all the knowledge of a critical system (the only network programmer), their departure causes major delays. Documentation research, pairing and cross-training reduce it.

## 11. Compensation

- **Salary bands** are in 13. Salaries have a market rate per role, level and location.
- **Tools:**
  - Annual raises (a review event every year).
  - Launch bonuses.
  - **Profit sharing** (a policy).
  - **Equity or options** (after incorporation; dilutes ownership).
  - Benefits (health, training budget).
- **Fairness:** employees compare themselves with peers. Paying a new hire more than a loyal veteran creates a morale event.

## 12. Employee requests and events

These are the brief's "employees approach the player with decisions". A sample of the deck:

| Event | Options | Consequences |
|---|---|---|
| **"Let's rewrite the inventory system."** | See decision card 2 in 05 | Tech debt; uncertain duration |
| **Feature pitch** | Add / prototype / decline | Scope creep versus morale |
| **Raise request** | Grant / partial raise + review in 6 months / refuse | A partial raise creates an internal promise. Refusing an underpaid person raises poaching risk. |
| **Promotion request** | Promote / not yet, with a development plan | The Peter principle, or an ambitious person's patience running out |
| **Crunch pushback** (team petition) | Negotiate / compensate / insist | Morale, Employee Reputation, possible unionization interest |
| **Conflict between two employees** | Mediate (founder blocks) / side with one / restructure / fire the Toxic one | Team morale, relationships |
| **Wants a different project or genre** | Transfer / keep them / offer creative time | Morale versus project continuity |
| **"Can I release my own small game on weekends?"** | Allow / forbid / **the studio publishes it** | Morale; possible distraction; the seed of a publishing label |
| **Remote work request** | Yes / hybrid / no | Retention versus culture (08) |
| **Burnout warning / sabbatical request** | Grant 1–3 months / deny | Lose them now, or risk collapse and quitting |
| **Poached** | See §10 | — |
| **Training or conference request** | Fund / deny | Skill, contacts, loyalty |
| **"I deleted the build."** (no source control) | Recover (partial) / accept the loss | A **problem-before-solution** event for source control (08) |
| **Controversial public post by an employee** | Support / distance / fire | Community reaction depends on context and tags |
| **Creative disagreement with your vision** | Listen and change / explain (Leadership check) / overrule | Morale versus vision cohesion |
| **Family leave** | Grant / pressure | Loyalty ++ / legal and reputation disaster |
| **Moonlighting for a rival discovered** | Talk / warn / fire | Trust, leaks |
| **Hero moment:** a QA tester finds a crash the night before certification | Reward / thank | Loyalty +, a story |
| **Idea pitch** | Add it to the notebook / prototype it | Personal Passion idea (04) |
| **Headhunted for a director role you don't have** | Create the role / support their move | Growth versus losing them |
| **Unionization discussion** (Tier 4+, triggered by crunch history, low pay or a large team) | Recognize / neutral / resist | Crunch limits and morale + / press risk and morale − |

## 13. Culture and policies

Policies are strategic identity. Together they form a **Culture profile** (for example "Sustainable & Transparent" or "Scrappy & Intense") that attracts certain candidates and repels others.

| Policy | Options | Tradeoffs |
|---|---|---|
| **Work hours** | 40 h / flexible / **4-day week** / always overtime | The 4-day week costs −15% output, which shrinks to about −8% after 3 months (a hidden focus effect). Burnout −, morale +, candidate appeal +. |
| **Crunch** | Never / emergencies only / as needed | Delivery flexibility versus reputation and turnover |
| **Remote** | On-site / hybrid / remote-first | See 08 |
| **Salary positioning** | −10% / market / +10% | Burn rate versus loyalty and candidate quality |
| **Profit sharing** | 0% / 5% / 10% of profits | Cost versus morale and loyalty. Pays out yearly. |
| **Equity pool** | 0–15% | Senior hiring versus dilution |
| **Creative time** | 0% / 10% | Output versus idea generation, Innovation and morale |
| **Hiring bar** | Fast / balanced / strict | Speed versus quality variance and trait surprises |
| **Transparency** | Closed / open finances | Trust and loyalty + / bigger morale shocks in bad times |
| **Learning budget** | $0 / $500 / $2,000 per person per year | XP and loyalty versus cost |

## 14. Layoffs and firing

- **Layoffs** cost severance (2–8 weeks of salary). They hit the remaining team's morale (survivor effect) and Employee Reputation, and may make news if the studio is well known.
- The player chooses **who**: named people with histories, which is intentionally uncomfortable. Seniority, performance and fairness are all visible considerations.
- **Firing for cause** (Toxic, chronically unreliable) costs less morale if the team already sees the problem. It can backfire if the team doesn't.
- **Alternatives:** voluntary pay cuts (a Leadership check; loyalty decides who stays), a 4-day week at 80% pay, pausing hiring, moving people onto contract work.

## 15. Relationships and chemistry

- Pairs of employees form **friendships** (shared success, crunch survived together) or **rivalries** (credit disputes, Hothead traits).
- Friends collaborate better (overhead −) and may leave together.
- Chemistry matters most in small teams, where one bad pairing can sink a project.

## 16. Multiple teams (late game)

- **Project teams** with a lead or producer. **Shared services:** a QA pool, audio, marketing, tech.
- **Allocation conflicts:** *"Both teams need the audio designer this month."*
- Team leads have decision styles, which gives each team a culture.
- **Cross-team transfers** reset familiarity and move knowledge.
- **Acquiring a small studio** (14) adds a ready-made team and IP, with **culture-clash** risk.
